6 Reasons Why Regular Dental Checkups Are Essential

Published on November 05, 2023 • By Dr. Smith

We all hear the recurring advice: "Visit your dentist every six months." But how important is it really, especially if your teeth feel fine? The truth is, waiting until you feel pain is one of the worst things you can do for your oral health—and your wallet.

Here are the top reasons why skipping your biannual dental checkup is a huge mistake.

1. Early Detection of Tooth Decay

By the time a cavity causes you pain, the decay has likely already breached the protective enamel and reached the sensitive inner dentin. Regular checkups, combined with X-rays, allow us to spot microscopic signs of decay early. This means we can treat a tiny cavity with a small filling before it turns into a painful—and expensive—root canal.

2. Plaque and Tartar Removal

Even with meticulous brushing and flossing, there are areas in your mouth that are incredibly difficult to clean. Plaque builds up in these blind spots and solidifies into tartar (calculus). Once tartar forms, you cannot brush it away; it requires professional scaling tools. Leaving tartar on your teeth leads directly to cavities and gum disease.

3. Preventing Gum Disease

Gum disease (periodontitis) is stealthy. It usually doesn't hurt until it's very advanced. Early symptoms like bleeding when you brush are often ignored. During a checkup, we measure the depth of the pockets around your teeth to check for early signs of gingivitis and periodontitis, saving your teeth from falling out later in life.

4. Oral Cancer Screenings

Oral cancer is highly treatable if caught early, but unfortunately, it is often diagnosed in its later stages. Every regular dental checkup includes an oral cancer screening. Your dentist checks your neck, jaw, lips, tongue, and the inside of your cheeks for any lumps, red or white patches, or abnormalities.

5. Checking Under the Surface with X-Rays

A visual inspection can only tell us so much. Dental X-rays allow us to see what's happening beneath the surface.

  • Impacted teeth (like wisdom teeth)
  • Bone decay caused by gum disease
  • Cysts or tumors
  • Decay between tight teeth contacts

6. Save Money in the Long Run

A routine cleaning and exam cost a fraction of what major restorative work costs. Treating a small cavity is cheap. Waiting until that cavity infects the pulp and requires a root canal and a crown is expensive. Preventative dentistry is always the most cost-effective approach.

When was your last checkup?

If it's been more than six months, you are overdue. Don't wait for pain to strike.
